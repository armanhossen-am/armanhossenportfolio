I subsequently worked at the Development Research Initiative as a Junior Officer in the Data Analytics Team, where I contributed to survey design, digital data collection systems, statistical modeling, and evidence-based decision-making for development and health projects. I also worked as a Research Intern at the BRAC James P Grant School of Public Health, supporting public health research and data-driven investigations.

My experience includes study design, survey development, data analysis, and the interpretation of findings for health and development initiatives. I am particularly interested in using quantitative methods to address real-world problems and generate insights that inform research, policy, and practice.

Research

My research focuses on applying statistical, machine learning, and deep learning methods to understand complex health and biological data. I am interested in transforming large-scale surveys and multi-omics datasets into meaningful insights that improve our understanding of disease, health outcomes, and population well-being. My work combines methodological rigor with practical applications in public health, epidemiology, and biomedical research.

  • Population Health and Epidemiology. Using large-scale national and international survey data to investigate health outcomes, health disparities, and the social, behavioral, and economic factors that influence population health.
  • Machine Learning and Predictive Modeling. Developing and evaluating predictive models using statistical learning, machine learning, and neural networks. My interests include model performance, interpretability, calibration, and the application of data-driven methods to health and biomedical problems.
  • Multi-Omics Data Integration and Computational Biology. Applying deep learning and data integration approaches to high-dimensional biological data, including RNA and miRNA expression datasets, to identify meaningful patterns and improve disease characterization.
  • Maternal, Reproductive, and Mental Health. Studying maternal health, reproductive health, anxiety, depression, postpartum depression, and related psychosocial factors, with a focus on identifying vulnerable populations and informing evidence-based interventions.
  • Non-Communicable Diseases and Risk Factors. Investigating hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, stroke, and related risk factors, as well as the demographic, behavioral, and environmental determinants associated with these conditions.
  • Reproducible and Transparent Research. Conducting reproducible analyses using R, Python, and Stata, with an emphasis on clear documentation, transparent workflows, and research practices that support verification and future reuse.
Current research project

Deep Learning for Multi-Omics Data Integration and Representation Learning

I am currently working on a research project that investigates deep learning approaches for integrating multi-omics datasets, including RNA and miRNA expression data. The project focuses on developing and evaluating neural network architectures for learning low-dimensional representations of high-dimensional biological data. Using autoencoders and representation learning techniques, the goal is to improve the identification of underlying biological patterns and enhance downstream tasks such as clustering, classification, and disease characterization. This work is being conducted as part of my graduate research in the Department of Statistics at Miami University.

Peer-reviewed journal articles

  1. Nahin, K. S. A., & Hossen, A. Pre-hypertension in Bangladesh: Evidence from BDHS 2022. Frontiers in Public Health, 14, 2026. Published

    Abstract

    Background: Hypertension drives premature mortality globally, yet its precursor, pre-hypertension (pre-HTN), remains under-researched in Bangladesh despite offering a critical window for intervention. This study examined the socio-demographic and health-related determinants of pre-hypertension among Bangladeshi adults and evaluated whether machine learning models provide additional predictive value beyond conventional approaches, in support of Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3.4.

    Methods: Pre-HTN was defined according to JNC 7 guidelines. Using the recent nationally representative sample from BDHS 2022, we integrated survey-weighted bivariate analyses and multivariable logistic regression to identify independent associations. A random forest classification model was implemented to assess variable importance and predictive performance. Model evaluation was conducted using confusion matrices and receiver operating characteristic curves.

    Results: Age and BMI were the most dominant predictors of pre-HTN, with the condition significantly more prevalent among the elderly and overweight individuals. Crucially, the crude association between diabetes and pre-HTN disappeared after multivariable adjustment. A reversal of the social gradient was observed, as higher education significantly increased risk (AOR = 1.26). Adults aged 60 years or older had nearly threefold higher odds compared with those aged under 30 (AOR = 2.75; 95% CI: 2.32-3.26). Regionally, residents of the coastal region exhibited a significantly higher prevalence of elevated blood pressure (AOR: 1.19; 95% CI: 1.04-1.36) compared to the central region. The random forest model ranked age, BMI, and sex as the most influential predictors. Discriminatory performance was modest and similar between models (AUC: logistic regression = 0.63; random forest = 0.62).

    Conclusion: Pre-hypertension in Bangladesh is shaped largely by aging, rising body weight, and shifting lifestyle patterns among the highly educated. Both logistic regression and random forest models pointed to the same core predictors, underscoring the stability of these findings. These results highlight the need for focused prevention strategies: weight control in older adults, movement integration into sedentary educated populations, and programmes tailored to regions where risk is consistently higher.

  2. Rony, A. R., Hossen, A., Asha, A. S., & Nahin, K. S. A. From Medical Necessity to Private-Sector Dominance: A Two-Decade Evolution of Caesarean Section Determinants in Bangladesh (2004 to 2022). Scientific Reports, 2026. View on Scholar

    Abstract

    Caesarean section (CS) prevalence in Bangladesh rose from approximately 4% in 2004 to nearly 45% in 2022, marking a dramatic transformation from a targeted life-saving procedure to a widespread practice. Using BDHS data on 26,845 ever-married women across three survey rounds (2004-2007, 2011-2014, and 2018-2022), this study documents how the socioeconomic and geographic determinants of CS delivery shifted over two decades. Variables were harmonised and administrative divisions grouped into broader regions to ensure comparability across rounds. The findings show that this shift has been driven predominantly by private-sector healthcare expansion and rising socioeconomic disparities, with an unregulated private sector identified as a central mechanism amplifying inequality in childbirth decisions.

  3. Nahin, K. S. A., Rony, A. R., Tasnim, S. J., Islam, T., Hossen, A., & Asha, A. S. The Hypertension Care Cascade in Bangladesh: A Nationally Representative Analysis of BDHS 2022. 2026. View on Scholar

    Abstract

  4. Islam, T., Rony, A. R., Nahin, K. S. A., Asha, A. S., Hossen, A., & Hossain, M. G. Socioeconomic Inequality and Postpartum Depression in Bangladesh: Evidence from BDHS 2022. Humanities and Social Sciences Communications, 2026. Under review

    Abstract

    Using data on 3,117 women aged 15-49 who had a live birth within the preceding 12 months in the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS), this study estimates the national prevalence of postpartum depressive symptoms and examines their individual- and community-level determinants. Depressive symptoms were measured using the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9; threshold score >= 5). A fully adjusted multilevel model shows that household wealth index is significantly associated with symptom burden, with women in lower wealth quintiles at higher risk. The study frames postpartum depression as an outcome embedded within broader socioeconomic and community environments and calls for wealth-sensitive mental health interventions for new mothers in Bangladesh.

  5. Hossen, A., et al. Reproductive Coercion as a Determinant of Anxiety and Depression Among Married Women in Bangladesh: A Nationally Representative Survey-Weighted Multinomial Analysis of the 2022 DHS. Archives of Women's Mental Health, 2026. Under review

    Abstract

    This study presents the first nationally representative analysis of anxiety and depression comorbidity among ever-married women in Bangladesh, investigating the role of reproductive coercion and structural powerlessness. Secondary data from 20,029 ever-married women in the 2022 Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS) were analysed. Mental health outcomes were classified into four categories (Neither, Anxiety-Only, Depression-Only, and Comorbid) using the validated Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7) and Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) instruments. Survey-weighted multinomial logistic regression was used to estimate Relative Risk Ratios (RRR). Overall, 15% of ever-married women had anxiety-only symptoms and 4.2% experienced comorbid anxiety and depression. Reproductive coercion was identified as a significant determinant of both single and comorbid mental health outcomes, underscoring the need for gender-sensitive mental health interventions in Bangladesh.

  6. Hossen, A., et al. The Paternal Shift in Child Survival: A Zero-Inflated Model of Socioeconomic Influence and Parental Education on Under-Five Deaths in Bangladesh. Scientific Reports, 2026. Under review

    Abstract

    This paper studies how parents' education and household circumstances relate to the number of under-five child deaths per mother in Bangladesh, using the 2022 BDHS. Because most mothers report no child deaths, it uses a zero-inflated model that handles the large share of zero counts, and it gives particular attention to the father's role alongside the mother's.
